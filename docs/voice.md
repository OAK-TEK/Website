# Voice

How OAK TEK writes on oaktek.org: page copy, buttons, alt text, meta tags and the
blog. This is tone and word choice; the visual side is in [theme.md](./theme.md) and
[design.md](./design.md).

## Principles

- **No em dashes. Ever.** Use a comma, colon, full stop or a separate sentence. This
  covers every visible string, `alt`, `aria-label`, `title`, meta tags, JSON-LD and
  this doc.
- **Plain and direct.** Short sentences. Say what the thing does, then stop. No
  hype, no "revolutionary", no "seamless".
- **Second person for visitors.** "You can delete your account in the app", not "Users
  may delete".
- **First person plural for the studio.** "We build what we use." We are a small
  studio, so write like people, not a corporation.
- **Prefer full forms.** "do not", "we are", "it is". Contractions are acceptable in
  casual blog copy but never in legal text or buttons.
- **Honest about status.** Say exactly what is live and where. Never promise a
  date for an unreleased app.
- **No fake urgency or superlatives.** No "best", "#1", "limited time".

## Who we are

OAK TEK is an independent app studio in Dubai, United Arab Emirates. We build
focused apps for hobbies, because we hit road bumps in our own hobbies and built
what we wished existed. Tagline: **Apps for the hobbies you love.**

## Status wording

| State | Say |
|---|---|
| Live on the stores | "Out now on Android and iOS." |
| Live on some platforms | "Live on Android and web, iOS coming soon." |
| Not live | "Coming soon". No date, no "in review", no countdown |
| Free | "Free, no subscription" only when true. Open Chord is supported by ads |

## Buttons and labels

- Verb first and short: "Get it free", "Send message", "See both apps".
- Store badges follow the store's own phrasing: "Get it on Google Play",
  "Download on the App Store".
- Link text says where it goes: "privacy policy", not "click here".

## Headlines

Short, sentence case, ends with a full stop when it is a statement.

- Good: "We build what we use." "Apps for the hobbies you love." "Learn it, see it, hear it."
- Avoid: title case, exclamation marks, questions as headlines.

## Meta descriptions

One sentence or two, under about 160 characters, no em dashes, no keyword stuffing.
Lead with what the page is. Keep the status line accurate ("Out now on Android and
iOS").

## Legal and privacy copy

- Never written or changed here. The approved text comes from the legal source
  repositories (see CLAUDE.md).
- Our own pages that point to legal text use neutral wording: "privacy policy",
  "terms", "Delete Your Account".
- The privacy facts we can state on marketing pages: progress stays on your device,
  the tuner listens on your device and what it hears is never uploaded, no account
  needed for Open Chord, analytics can be turned off. Do not state anything stronger.

## Errors and status messages

Say what happened and what to do next, calmly.

- "Thank you. Your message has been sent, and we will reply by email."
- "We could not send that here, so we are opening your email app instead."

## Blog

Written for a beginner who wants the answer. Open with the answer, then explain.
Link to the apps only where they truly help. Date and byline every post.

## Checklist before publishing

- [ ] No em dashes anywhere, including attributes
- [ ] `OAK TEK - FZCO` where the legal entity is named
- [ ] Store status matches reality and no dates for unreleased apps
- [ ] Reads aloud in one pass without hype
